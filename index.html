01  /  The 80-year stagnation Tube and wing

Freight has been flying in the wrong airframe.

Cargo aircraft design has barely moved in close to a century. Most freighters in service today started life as passenger aircraft and were converted to carry boxes instead of people.

02  /  A new airframe for freight

Built for freight.
From the ground up.

More volume, more payload

The blended wing body carries more internal volume and more payload than a tube-and-wing aircraft of similar size.

Whatever needs to move

03  /  Integration of hydrogen propulsion

The LH2-BWB advantage for cargo

Volume solution

Liquid hydrogen is bulky. The blended body has room for cryogenic tanks while the cargo bay stays completely unobstructed.

Range extender

A high lift-to-drag ratio and very light fuel offset the weight of the cryogenic system, so range grows without giving up payload.

Cargo advantage

With no passenger requirements to design around, the whole interior goes to tank integration and cargo capacity.

04  /  Energy density

Roughly three times the energy in every kilogram.

Liquid hydrogen carries about 3x the energy per kilogram of jet fuel, which means lighter take-off weights for the same mission.

Liquid hydrogen 120 MJ/kg
Jet fuel, A-1 43.2 MJ/kg

Energy independence

A water-to-fuel ecosystem. Electrolysis and liquefaction happen on site, so operators are far less exposed to swings in the oil price.

Cost stability

Hydrogen is less tied to oil markets. As electrolyzers and liquefiers get more efficient and supply grows, the levelized cost of hydrogen is expected to fall substantially over the coming years and decades.

06  /  Tactical advantage

H2 BWB aircraft for defense logistics

Low thermal signature

Fuel cells run cooler than gas turbines, which cuts the aircraft's infrared signature.

Noise reduction

Propulsion mounted above the airframe keeps noise down on sensitive missions.

Resilient supply

Forward bases can make hydrogen from water using deployable solar or nuclear power. No fuel convoys.
